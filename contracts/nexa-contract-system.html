NexaStrategic Ltd · Contract system · Draft 0.1

Fewer documents.
Clearer terms.

One set of standard terms you rarely touch, a two-page engagement order for each client, and a schedule for each of the three seats.

The design principleThe client negotiates one page, the cover. Everything behind it is standard and stays standard. That keeps deals fast and stops you running twelve slightly different contracts.
Architecture

Three layers. Each one changes at a different speed.

UK buyers already know this structure: an order form sitting on top of standard terms. It's the same one the Crown Commercial Service uses for G-Cloud call-off contracts, so procurement teams recognise it straight away. If the layers conflict, the engagement order wins over the schedule, and the schedule wins over the standard terms.

Layer 1 · Fixed

Terms of Business

The legal engine: status, IP, confidentiality, liability, data, termination, law and jurisdiction. Published at a versioned URL (nexastrategic.com/terms/v1) and incorporated by reference.

Changes: once a year, by you, never per client
Layer 2 · Per client

Engagement Order

The cover page. Parties, seat, start date, commitment, fees, notice, cap and any agreed variations, all in one key-terms grid. This is the only page that gets signed and redlined.

Changes: every engagement
Layer 3 · Per seat

Seat Schedule

Defines the role: scope, authority, deliverables and what is out of scope. Three versions, matching the three seats on the website.

Changes: rarely; you pick one, two or all three
Schedule A · Monthly retainer from £5,000Fractional COO.

Inside the business. Operational ownership, delegated spend authority with limits, management rhythm, people, delivery and risk.

Schedule B · £300 per active client per monthResident Operations Adviser.

Embedded support with no executive authority. The fee moves with the number of customers the client is actively serving, and the rate is reviewed when that reaches 50.

Schedule C · Hourly, block paid in advanceOperations Adviser.

Advice on request. The client buys a monthly block of hours, time is recorded in 15-minute units, and extra hours are billed with the next invoice.

Mutual NDA · for the scoping stage, before any order Data Processing Addendum · UK GDPR Art. 28, used when you touch employee data Change Note · one page to vary scope or days mid-engagement Associate Agreement · back-to-back terms for anyone you subcontract

Status & authority

6.1

Nexa provides the Services as an independent business on its own account. Nothing in the Agreement makes Nexa or the Key Person an employee, worker, partner or agent of the Client.

In plain EnglishWe're a supplier, not staff.
6.2

Nexa has no authority to bind the Client, sign on its behalf or incur expenditure except within the written limits set out in the applicable Seat Schedule.

In plain EnglishAny authority is written down and capped.
6.3

The Client must not require the Key Person to act as, or hold themselves out as, a director. The Client's board keeps responsibility for every decision, and Nexa's recommendations are advisory only.

In plain EnglishThe board decides. This keeps Mila from being treated as a shadow director.

Liability

7.1

Nothing in the Agreement limits liability for death or personal injury caused by negligence, for fraud or fraudulent misrepresentation, or for any other liability that cannot lawfully be limited.

In plain EnglishSome things can't be capped under English law.
7.2

Subject to clause 7.1, Nexa is not liable for any of the following: (a) loss of profits; (b) loss of revenue, business or anticipated savings; (c) loss of goodwill; or (d) any indirect or consequential loss.

In plain EnglishEach excluded loss is named separately, which is what the courts expect.

Subject to clause 7.1, Nexa's total aggregate liability for all claims under or in connection with the Agreement is limited to the total Fees paid and payable in the 12 months before the first claim arose.

Drafting positions

Where Nexa starts every negotiation.

These are my proposed defaults for the standard terms. Your heads of terms will override any of them, which is the point of seeing them now.

ClauseNexa defaultWhy
Parties & disclosuresEach party is described in full: registered name, place of registration, company number and registered office. The same details appear in the footer of every page.The Company, Limited Liability Partnership and Business (Names and Trading Disclosures) Regulations 2015 require order forms and business documents to show the registered name, number, place of registration and registered office.
Contracting entityNexaStrategic Ltd supplies, with you named as Key Person. You never contract personally.This keeps the limited liability shield and the outside-IR35 structure intact. Substitution is limited to approved associates with client consent, because a pure substitution right isn't credible for a named COO and HMRC tests working practices, not paper.
IR35A medium or large client must issue a reasoned Status Determination Statement before work starts. Nexa can dispute it, and the client must respond within 45 days. Each side indemnifies the other for its own errors.These are the off-payroll rules in Chapter 10 of ITEPA 2003. Since 6 April 2026, a company counts as small if it meets two of these three tests: turnover up to £15m, balance sheet up to £7.5m, 50 or fewer employees. About 14,000 more clients now fall outside the rules, and for them NexaStrategic makes the determination. The contract also has to match how you actually work (own tools, no line management, control over method), because HMRC looks at practice, not paper.
AuthorityNo authority unless the Seat Schedule sets it out, with a monetary limit. The client extends its D&O insurance to cover you where its policy allows.Sections 250 and 251 of the Companies Act 2006 catch de facto and shadow directors, meaning anyone the board habitually follows. That is the real exposure for the Build and Govern seats.
LiabilityClause 7.1 sets out what can't be capped. Clause 7.2 lists each excluded loss by name. Clause 7.3 is one aggregate cap at 12 months' fees, with a floor of the first year's fees so the cap still means something in month one.Under UCTA 1977 the cap must be reasonable, and tying it to fees and your PI cover supports that. In Soteria v IBM [2022] the court read "consequential loss" narrowly, so lost profits must be excluded by name. Drax v Wipro [2023] shows how much litigation an unclear "single cap or per-claim cap" wording can cause.
Fees & paymentA monthly retainer, invoiced in advance plus VAT and payable within 14 days. Late payment carries statutory interest and fixed compensation under the 1998 Act.The Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998 gives 8% over Bank of England base rate, plus £40, £70 or £100 per invoice depending on its size. The Commercial Payments Bill, now in Parliament, would cap terms at 60 days. Fourteen days is already well inside that.
VATAll Fees are exclusive of VAT, which the Client pays on top at the rate in force on the invoice date. If Nexa becomes VAT-registered during an Engagement, VAT is added from the effective date of registration. UK invoices show Nexa's VAT number. Invoices to overseas business clients are marked "outside the scope of UK VAT".If a contract says nothing about VAT, the price is treated as VAT-inclusive, and you lose a sixth of the fee to HMRC. Section 89 of the VAT Act 1994 isn't a reliable fix for a mid-contract registration, so the clause covers it expressly. Advisory services supplied business-to-business to a US client are normally taxed where the customer is, so no UK VAT is charged.
Term & exitA 3 to 6 month initial term, then rolling on 60 days' notice. Either side can terminate immediately for material breach. Billing in advance is the real protection against insolvency.Under section 233B of the Insolvency Act 1986, added by CIGA 2020, a supplier usually can't terminate just because the client enters an insolvency procedure. Your insolvency termination right is largely unenforceable against a client. Billing in advance means you're never owed for work already done.
IPThe client owns deliverables once paid. Nexa keeps its frameworks, templates and know-how (Background IP), and grants the client a perpetual internal-use licence.Your methodology is the business. Without a Background IP carve-out, an assignment of deliverables arguably takes the method with it.
Non-solicitMutual, lasting 6 months, and limited to people who worked on the engagement. The remedy is a placement fee rather than an injunction, and there is a severance clause.Under Kores v Kolok [1959], wide no-poach clauses between businesses are unenforceable. Tillman v Egon Zehnder [2019] allows a court to strike out the bad part rather than the whole clause. The CMA has also warned that broad no-poach agreements can breach the Competition Act 1998, so keep this one narrow and tied to the engagement.
DataA Data Processing Addendum is attached whenever you process staff or customer personal data. Transfers to the US rely on the UK–US Data Bridge, or on the IDTA for US recipients that aren't certified.The law here is UK GDPR Article 28, the Data Protection Act 2018, and the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 amendments. People work means HR data, and a missing processor contract is a gap that diligence will find.
ComplianceMutual anti-bribery, anti-slavery and anti-facilitation-of-tax-evasion warranties, plus a fraud-prevention clause.The relevant laws are the Bribery Act 2010 (s.7), Modern Slavery Act 2015, Criminal Finances Act 2017, and the ECCTA 2023 offence of failure to prevent fraud, in force since 1 September 2025. Large clients' procurement teams will ask for these clauses, so have them ready.
BoilerplateEntire agreement with non-reliance, which can't exclude fraud. Third-party rights are excluded. Notices by email have defined deemed receipt, but legal proceedings can't be served by email. Severance, variation in writing only, and no waiver.Section 3 of the Misrepresentation Act 1967 makes non-reliance wording subject to a reasonableness test. Excluding the Contracts (Rights of Third Parties) Act 1999 stops a client's investors or group companies suing you directly.
Law & disputesThe law of England and Wales, with exclusive jurisdiction for its courts. Disputes go to founder escalation within 15 Business Days, then CEDR mediation, before anyone issues proceedings.Churchill v Merthyr Tydfil [2023] confirmed that courts can order parties into ADR, so a clear, staged clause gets enforced. For US clients the terms stay English initially, and we add a New York or Delaware variant once there's a US entity.
Design rules

The brand, translated to paper.

Colour

The page is paper and the text is ink. Wine is for structure: clause numbers, margin notes and the closing block. Pink appears only as the full stop on the wordmark and headings. Blush is used for rules only.

Paper #F5EFE7Ink #191716Wine #5A202BBlush #DDB5B0Pink #D94C86

Type

Roca One for titles, seat names and the two headline numbers. Neue Montreal for everything else, with clause body at 9.5pt and 1.5 leading. Display type never goes in operative wording.

Two file formats

The negotiation copy is a Word file in a safe font (Arial or Helvetica), because counterparties redline in Word and won't have Roca One installed. The executed copy is a branded PDF built from the same text. Contracts have to survive editing by other people, which is why you need both.

Numbering & references

Use one level of decimal numbering (6.1, 6.2), with lettered lists (a), (b) inside a clause, and avoid 6.1.2(a)(iii). Cross-references use a lower-case "clause 7.1". Defined terms are bold at first use and capitalised after. Each order gets a reference (NX-YYYY-NNN) in the running head of every page.

UK drafting conventions

Use British spelling and day-month-year dates (3 November 2026). Write "must" for obligations and "may" for permissions, avoiding "shall", in line with current UK plain-English practice. Use the defined term Business Day, meaning not a Saturday, Sunday or bank holiday in England. Write £ amounts in figures and always say whether VAT is included.

Page & execution

Set pages on A4 with 25mm margins, page numbers in the form "x / y", and the trading disclosures in the footer. The contract is a simple contract signed by an authorised signatory, not a deed, so no witness is needed. Electronic signatures are valid under section 7 of the Electronic Communications Act 2000, as the Law Commission confirmed in 2019.

Plain-English notes

Margin notes are a reading aid and aren't operative (clause 1.4). They must never add anything the clause doesn't say. They are written last, from the final text, and checked at every revision.

Before I draft

What I need alongside the heads of terms.

1
Registered details: done. NexaStrategic Ltd, company number 15536373, registered office 7 Bell Yard, London, England, WC2A 2JR.
2
Pricing: done. Fractional COO from £5,000 a month, Resident Operations Adviser at £300 per active client, Operations Adviser hourly. All invoiced in advance unless agreed otherwise.
3
Equity for the Govern seat. Options, growth shares or an advisory share agreement? EMI isn't available to non-employees, so this changes Schedule C.
4
Your PI insurance limit. The liability cap should sit at or below it.
5
VAT registration date and number. Fees will be quoted plus VAT. I also need the effective date of registration, so any engagement already running picks up VAT from the right day.
6
US plans. Do you have or expect a US entity? That decides whether we build the US variant now or later.
7
Associates. Do you ever bring in other people? If so, the Associate Agreement becomes part of the first build.
8
Signing tool. DocuSign, Dropbox Sign or PandaDoc? Each sets how the cover page fields need to be laid out.
