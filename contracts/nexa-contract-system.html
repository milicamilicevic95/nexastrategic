NexaStrategic Ltd · Contract system · Draft 0.1

Fewer documents.
Clearer terms.

One set of standard terms you rarely touch, a two-page engagement order for each client, and a schedule for each of the three seats.

The design principleThe client negotiates one page, the cover. Everything behind it is standard and stays standard. That keeps deals fast and stops you running twelve slightly different contracts.
Architecture

Three layers. Each one changes at a different speed.

This is the cover-page model Common Paper and Bonterms popularised for SaaS, adapted for advisory. If the layers conflict, the engagement order wins over the schedule, and the schedule wins over the standard terms.

Layer 1 · Fixed

Terms of Business

The legal engine: status, IP, confidentiality, liability, data, termination, law and jurisdiction. Published at a versioned URL (nexastrategic.com/terms/v1) and incorporated by reference.

Changes: once a year, by you, never per client
Layer 2 · Per client

Engagement Order

The cover page. Parties, seat, start date, commitment, fees, notice, cap and any agreed variations, all in one key-terms grid. This is the only page that gets signed and redlined.

Changes: every engagement
Layer 3 · Per seat

Seat Schedule

Defines the role: scope, authority, deliverables and what is out of scope. Three versions, matching the three seats on the website.

Changes: rarely; you pick one, two or all three
Schedule A · Fractional COOBuild.

Inside the business. Operational ownership, delegated authority with limits, management rhythm, people, delivery and risk.

Schedule B · Operating PartnerChallenge.

Alongside the founders. Advisory only, with no executive authority. Covers decision support, sequencing and capacity.

Schedule C · Board AdvisorGovern.

Around the board table. A non-director advisory seat with meeting cadence, papers and an optional equity mechanism.

Mutual NDA · for the scoping stage, before any order Data Processing Addendum · UK GDPR Art. 28, used when you touch employee data Change Note · one page to vary scope or days mid-engagement Associate Agreement · back-to-back terms for anyone you subcontract

CLAUSE 6

Status & authority

6.1

Nexa provides the Services as an independent business on its own account. Nothing in the Agreement makes Nexa or the Key Person an employee, worker, partner or agent of the Client.

In plain EnglishWe're a supplier, not staff.
6.2

Nexa has no authority to bind the Client, sign on its behalf or incur expenditure except within the written limits set out in the applicable Seat Schedule.

In plain EnglishAny authority is written down and capped.
6.3

The Client shall not require the Key Person to act as, or hold themselves out as, a director. The Client's board retains responsibility for all decisions, and Nexa's recommendations are advisory.

In plain EnglishThe board decides. This keeps Mila from being treated as a shadow director.

The Client shall extend its directors' and officers' insurance to cover the Key Person for the duration of the Engagement, where its policy permits.

In plain EnglishIf you put Mila in the room, your D&O policy covers her too.

Drafting positions

Where Nexa starts every negotiation.

These are my proposed defaults for the standard terms. Your heads of terms will override any of them, which is the point of seeing them now.

ClauseNexa defaultWhy
Contracting entityNexaStrategic Ltd supplies, with you named as Key Person. You never contract personally.This keeps the limited liability shield and the outside-IR35 structure intact. Substitution is limited to approved associates with client consent, because a pure substitution right isn't credible for a named COO and HMRC tests working practices, not paper.
IR35The client issues a Status Determination Statement if it's medium or large. Each side indemnifies for its own errors.Since April 2021, medium and large private-sector clients carry the determination duty. For small clients the decision stays with your company, and the contract should reflect how you actually work: own tools, no line management, control over method.
AuthorityNone unless the Seat Schedule sets it out, with a monetary limit.Section 251 of the Companies Act 2006 creates shadow director risk for anyone whose instructions the board habitually follows. That is the real exposure for the Build and Govern seats.
LiabilityCapped at 12 months' fees. Indirect and consequential loss and lost profits are excluded. Fraud, death and personal injury are carved out.This cap is market-standard for advisory work and matches what PI insurers expect. Death, injury and fraud can't be excluded under UCTA 1977 anyway.
Fees & paymentMonthly retainer invoiced in advance and payable in 14 days. Statutory interest applies to late payment.Billing in advance funds the month you're working. The Late Payment of Commercial Debts (Interest) Act 1998 gives you 8% over base, so the clause just points to it.
Term & exitA 3 to 6 month initial term, then rolling on 60 days' notice. Either side can terminate immediately for material breach or insolvency.Operating work needs a runway, because month one is diagnosis. The minimum term protects that, and the rolling notice stops the contract feeling like a trap.
IPThe client owns deliverables once paid. Nexa keeps its frameworks, templates and know-how, and grants the client a licence to use them internally.Your methodology is the business. Without a retained IP clause, every engagement arguably hands it over.
Non-solicitMutual and limited to 6 months. Team members introduced by Nexa are covered, and a placement fee applies.Six months is enforceable. Twelve months or more invites a restraint-of-trade challenge for a services relationship.
DataA DPA is attached whenever you process staff or customer personal data.People work means HR data. A missing Article 28 contract is a compliance gap on the client's side, and they'll notice it in diligence.
Law & disputesEnglish law and the English courts, with a 30-day escalation step to founders first.This works for UK clients. For US clients the terms stay English initially, and we add a New York or Delaware variant once you have a US entity or US-heavy revenue.
Design rules

The brand, translated to paper.

Colour

The page is paper and the text is ink. Wine is for structure: clause numbers, margin notes and the closing block. Pink appears only as the full stop on the wordmark and headings. Blush is used for rules only.

Paper #F5EFE7Ink #191716Wine #5A202BBlush #DDB5B0Pink #D94C86

Type

Roca One for titles, seat names and the two headline numbers. Neue Montreal for everything else, with clause body at 9.5pt and 1.5 leading. Display type never goes in operative wording.

Two file formats

The negotiation copy is a Word file in a safe font (Arial or Helvetica), because counterparties redline in Word and won't have Roca One installed. The executed copy is a branded PDF built from the same text. Contracts have to survive editing by other people, which is why you need both.

Numbering & references

Use one level of decimal numbering (6.1, 6.2) and avoid 6.1.2(a)(iii). Defined terms are bold at first use and capitalised after. Each order gets a reference (NX-YYYY-NNN) in the running head of every page.

Before I draft

What I need alongside the heads of terms.

1
Exact registered name and number. The site says “NexaStrategic Ltd”. You said “Nexa Strategic”. The contract must match Companies House exactly.
2
Pricing model per seat. Is it a retainer for a set number of days, a day rate, or a fixed fee per phase? Board Advisor may be a retainer plus equity.
3
Equity for the Govern seat. Options, growth shares or an advisory share agreement? EMI isn't available to non-employees, so this changes Schedule C.
4
Your PI insurance limit. The liability cap should sit at or below it.
5
VAT status and whether fees are quoted exclusive of VAT.
6
US plans. Do you have or expect a US entity? That decides whether we build the US variant now or later.
7
Associates. Do you ever bring in other people? If so, the Associate Agreement becomes part of the first build.
8
Signing tool. DocuSign, Dropbox Sign or PandaDoc? Each sets how the cover page fields need to be laid out.
