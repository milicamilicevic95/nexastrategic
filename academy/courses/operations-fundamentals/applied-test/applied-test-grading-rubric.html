Grading rubric.

Six questions, each Ready or Not yet. No scores. The result is Ready only if every question that applies is Ready. Otherwise it’s Not yet, with one thing to redo.

QuestionReady looks likeNot yet looks likeTypical “one thing to redo”
1 · All eight partsEvery part has a specific line. A part that genuinely needs nothing says so, and why.A part is missing, marked n/a with no reason, or merged with another (“legal and compliance: get advice”).“Add Compliance: what has to be true about attendee data and insurance before Kestrel says yes.”
2 · Only this businessUses the facts: names, numbers, contracts. Part A mentions things like Client C at 48%, the 30% deposits, Marcus at capacity. Part B could not be about anyone else’s company.Generic lines that fit any business: “ensure robust processes”, “hire the right people”, “monitor cash closely”.“Rewrite the Finance line using Kestrel’s numbers: what happens to the lowest week once January’s deposits go in?”
3 · Now apart from nextNow holds only true preconditions (cash, contract terms, who runs delivery, who signs). Next holds what can wait. The now list is short.Everything is now; or something that blocks the decision (cash, contract, capacity) has been pushed to next; or no time frame at all.“Move the cash question to now: Kestrel can’t pay January deposits from a forecast that already goes negative.”
4 · A name on every partOne person per line, not a team. The founder appears only where it has to be them. Part B says who holds the whole, or when that changes.“The team”, “TBC”, two names on one line, or the founder on every line with no plan to change it.“Put one name on Governance. If it has to be you, say who takes it in six months.”
5 · The decision moved, with a limitPart B: the handover names the decision as well as the task, a clear limit, when they stop and ask, a lock-in date four weeks on, and a weekly check of the record. Part A credit if they deal with who signs Manchester venue contracts.“Use your judgement.” A task with no decision, or no limit. No lock-in date. Evidence that the founder is still re-doing the work.“Add the limit: what can Sam approve alone, up to how much, and what makes them stop and ask?”
6 · A specialist could act on itThe right specialist. The situation in three or four sentences, what’s already decided, one precise question, a deadline, no client names.“Please review our contract.” Several questions at once, no deadline, or asking the specialist to make the business decision.“Cut the brief to one question the accountant can answer this week, and give them the numbers they’ll need.”

Choosing the one thing

If several are Not yet, pick the one that would most change their answer, in this order: 3 (a blocker deferred) → 1 (a part missing) → 4 (no names) → 5 (decision didn’t move) → 6 (brief not usable) → 2 (generic). Make it specific enough to act on this week. Mention the others in a line if you like, but only one is the redo.

Part A · answer guide

What a strong answer to Kestrel spots

They don’t need all of this. A Ready answer catches the blockers (cash, Client C’s terms, who runs Manchester, who signs) and has something specific in every part. Credit anything sensible that isn’t listed.

The specialist brief

Any specialist is fine if the brief is precise. The strongest choices are the accountant (the cash gap and how to fund deposits), the lawyer (the Client C amendment and Manchester venue contracts) or an insurance broker (cover in a new city).

A strong “yes, once…”: Yes, once Client C agrees a deposit and the cash view holds with the Manchester deposits in it, someone other than Marcus is running Manchester, and Manchester venue contracts can be signed without Alex.
A Not yet: “Yes, it’s a great opportunity. We need to make sure we have the right people and processes, and keep an eye on cash.” (Fails 2 and 3: generic, and nothing is a precondition.)

Part B · and how to run the review

What to look for in their own business

  • Now should match what their module 1 map said, or explain what changed. A page of “Owned” with no evidence is a flag.
  • Next is one change per part in the next three months, not a wish list.
  • Who holds the whole: “me, for now” is fine if they say what changes and when.
  • Rhythm: should include the 13-week cash view weekly and the compliance calendar at least monthly. If it doesn’t, that’s worth a line.
  • The handover is the core of Part B. It only counts once it has stayed off their plate for four weeks: check the date and the evidence when it arrives.

Running it

  • About 20 minutes per founder. Read Part A, then Part B, then fill in the review sheet.
  • Write the result first, then the one thing to redo, then one line on what they did well.
  • Not yet means resubmit that one piece only, not the whole test. Turn resubmissions around within a week.
  • The handover evidence often lands after the rest. Mark question 5 as pending until it does.

Feedback that lands

Good: “Ready on everything except 3. Move the cash question to now: with the deposits in, Kestrel’s lowest week goes further below zero. What you did well: the Governance line is exactly right, and it’s the one most people miss.”
Too vague: “Good start, but think more about cash and be more specific in places.”
