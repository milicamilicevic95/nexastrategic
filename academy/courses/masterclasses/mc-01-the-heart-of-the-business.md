## Learning objective

By the end, a founder can explain in plain language what operations does for their business, recognise which of four failure conditions they have, and run a four-question heart check on their own business this week.

## Takeaway

The four-question heart check:

1. What is waiting on me right now? *(blockage)*
2. When did we last check it properly? *(rhythm)*
3. What cash will we have in two months? *(pump)*
4. Who, if gone tomorrow, stops us? *(sudden event)*

## The idea

Every business has a **brain**: the founder, with the ideas, decisions and direction. It has a **body**: what customers see, the product, the service, the brand. What connects them is the **heart**: operations. It moves what the business needs (decisions, money, information and people) to every part, on a rhythm. A brilliant brain cannot keep a body alive on its own. When the heart isn't set, the rest is a mess.

The four failure conditions follow the metaphor, and they become the diagnostic:

| Condition | What it feels like | Signs |
|---|---|---|
| **Blockage** | Everything waits for you | Approvals queue up · the team asks before acting · holidays stall the business |
| **No rhythm** | Nothing happens on a beat | Numbers checked only when there's a worry · renewals found when they lapse · every week feels different |
| **A weak pump** | The money can't reach where it's needed | Profitable but short of cash · paid in 60 days, paying in 14 · growth makes it worse |
| **The sudden event** | It's fine, until it isn't | A key person leaves · a big client goes · a contract surprises you |

## Section notes

### 1. The picture (10 minutes)

Build the picture one slide at a time: brain, body, then the question "what connects them?" Land on the heart. Then the four things it moves, and the diagram. Keep it plain; the metaphor does the work. End on the pink statement slide and pause.

### 2. What failure looks like (20 minutes)

One slide per condition. For each: say the condition, read the signs, tell one short anonymised story from your own work, and ask for a show of hands.

**Your stories go here.** Use real, anonymised examples from your own work, with no client names and nothing that identifies a business. If you'd rather not, these fictional illustrations work:

- **Blockage:** [Your story] *Fallback:* a founder who approves every quote. While they're at a three-day conference, nothing is quoted and two leads go elsewhere.
- **No rhythm:** [Your story] *Fallback:* a team finds out an insurance policy lapsed only when they need to claim.
- **A weak pump:** [Your story] *Fallback:* a profitable agency can't make payroll one month because three clients pay late at once.
- **The sudden event:** [Your story] *Fallback:* the one person who ran operations leaves, and nobody else has the passwords or knows when the renewals are.

### 3. Why it's underestimated (10 minutes)

Three reasons, then one slide that ties to the course: businesses cut the heart into ops teams (legal ops, fin ops, rev ops), and each team sees only its own colour of the ball. Mention that Operations Fundamentals teaches the ball in full. Don't teach the eight panels here; that's the course.

### 4. Live case (25 minutes)

Ask for a volunteer with a real decision they're facing (a hire, a new client, a new market). Run the four heart-check questions with the room. Keep it anonymous if they prefer. If nobody volunteers, use the prepared case.

**Prepared case (fictional).** Sam runs a 12-person events agency and wants to open in a second city.

| Check | What the room should find |
|---|---|
| Blockage | Every quote and supplier contract goes through Sam |
| Rhythm | Nobody looks at the numbers weekly; Sam checks the bank on Fridays |
| Pump | Venues need deposits up front; clients pay 30 days after the event. A second city doubles the deposits |
| Sudden event | One producer holds every venue relationship |

**Where it should land:** the second city is possible, but only after a quote sign-off limit for the team, a weekly cash view, renegotiated deposit or payment terms, and a second person on venue relationships. The answer is "yes, once the heart can carry it", not "no".

### 5. This week (10 minutes)

The four questions, one per condition. Ask everyone to write their answers before they leave. Close on the wine slide, then point to Operations Fundamentals: "start with the ball".

## Repurposing

- **Elevate LinkedIn Live (30 minutes):** sections 1, 2 and 5, with one story per condition and no live case. For an early-career audience, add: operations is where you learn how a business actually works.
- **Keynote (20 minutes):** the picture, the four conditions and the four questions.
