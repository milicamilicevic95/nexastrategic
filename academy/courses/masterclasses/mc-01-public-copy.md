## 1. Website page

**Page title:** Know Enough

**Headline:** Bookkeeper, lawyer or ops hire? Know what your business needs now, and next.

**Intro**

Most founders think operations means process. Process is one part of it. Operations is the whole ball: process, people, commercial, finance, legal, compliance, governance and risk.

You don't need to become the operations person. You need to know enough to know when to bring someone in, who to bring in, and what to ask them.

**Two tests**

**Now: the house isn't on fire.** You understand what's happening, and the company can exist.

**Next: if you stepped away, what breaks?** And where would you still need a person's judgement?

Most founders live in Now. Once you have paying clients in a clear market and comfortable runway, the job changes: you need a written plan, and every part of the business has to answer to it.

**What you leave with**

- The Know Enough Map: eight parts of your business, scored at Now and Next
- Who could help with each one, starting with the smallest step
- A four-part brief, so you get a precise answer rather than a big bill

**About**

Know Enough is a monthly masterclass series by Mila Milićević, fractional COO at NexaStrategic. Mila works across the whole of operations and knows exactly where her limits are. The series teaches founders to do the same.

## 2. LinkedIn post

**Content pillar:** Opinion / Authority
**Format:** Text post. Pair it with the carousel a few days later.
**Target audience:** Founders, pre-seed to Series A

---

Most founders think operations is process.

It isn't. Process is one panel of eight.

People. Finance. Legal. Commercial. Compliance. Governance. Risk. And process.

I'm a fractional COO, so my job is to know a bit about all eight, and exactly where my limits are.

When I go to a lawyer, I don't ask them to tell me everything. I ask one question.

When someone tells a founder they need a CFO, I know whether what they need is a good accountant.

But here's the thing: founders don't need to become the operator.

You need to know enough to know when.

I use two tests.

NOW: the house isn't on fire. You understand what's happening, and the company can exist.

NEXT: if you stepped away for a month, what would break? And where would you still need a person's judgement?

Most founders only ever run the first test.

The second one tells you who to bring in, and when.

Which test would your business fail first?
