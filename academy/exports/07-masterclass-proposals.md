## SOP clinic: what to document first, and where it should live

> Status: Proposal · no date agreed

**Purpose**

Answer the documentation questions founders have already raised (what SOPs are, where they live, how an SOP hub works, screen-capture guides versus written SOPs) by working through learners' real examples.

**Course outcomes:** M5.1 Decide whether and how to document; M5.2 Distinguish capturing steps from designing a process; M5.3 Evaluate an SOP hub or documentation tool

**Linked modules:** Documentation and SOPs that people use

**Suggested format**

Short framing (10 minutes), then two or three volunteered examples worked through live using the decision guide, then Q&A. Learners bring one task they are unsure about.

**Practical takeaway**

A completed documentation decision for one real task, and a keep, change or stop view on their current documentation location.

**Specialist guest**

Optional. A practitioner who runs documentation in a small business could add a second perspective. Not a tool vendor. (Would add value)

## Why it still comes back to you: the founder side of the bottleneck (with David)

> Status: Proposal · no date agreed

**Purpose**

Pair the operational diagnosis from Module 3 with David's perspective on founder behaviour, so learners see both the structural and the personal reasons work returns to them.

**Course outcomes:** M3.1 Diagnose bottleneck types with evidence; M3.2 Ask diagnostic questions

**Linked modules:** Why founders become the bottleneck

**Suggested format**

Conversation between Mila and David (30 minutes) using one anonymised case, then learners diagnose their own using the bottleneck diagnostic, then discussion.

**Practical takeaway**

A diagnosis that separates structural causes (decision rights, knowledge, visibility) from behavioural ones, with one action for each.

**Specialist guest**

David Jacob as co-presenter. Not yet agreed. (Would add value)

## Decision rights in practice: building your approval thresholds

> Status: Proposal · no date agreed

**Purpose**

Help learners turn Module 4 into a working decision rights table for their own business, and test it against realistic situations.

**Course outcomes:** M4.1 Specify owner, decision rights, threshold and escalation

**Linked modules:** Ownership, decisions and when to get advice

**Suggested format**

Worked example (15 minutes), learners draft five rows, then small-group stress test: 'what happens if…' scenarios, then debrief.

**Practical takeaway**

A five-row decision rights table with thresholds and escalation, ready to trial for a month.

**Specialist guest**

Optional. An experienced COO or finance director could add perspective on thresholds in growing companies. (Not needed)

## From signed agreement to delivery: reading a contract for its operational consequences

> Status: Proposal · no date agreed

**Purpose**

Extend Module 4 Lesson 4.3. Learners practise spotting the operational and commercial consequences in a sample agreement, and learn what to take to a solicitor.

**Course outcomes:** M4.2 Identify consequences of a commitment; M4.3 Recognise when specialist advice is needed; M1.2 Trace a promise to capacity, margin and cash

**Linked modules:** Ownership, decisions and when to get advice; What operations is, and why it is commercial

**Suggested format**

A fictional sample agreement is reviewed together for scope, delivery commitments, service credits, payment terms and dependencies. A specialist explains what they would want to see in an advice request. Q&A on recognition, not individual advice.

**Practical takeaway**

A completed commitment check on the sample agreement, and a template advice brief.

**Specialist guest**

A commercial solicitor would add significant value, to explain what needs qualified review and how to brief them. They should not give individual advice in the session. (Would add value)

## What a founder should see each week: money, capacity and obligations

> Status: Proposal · no date agreed

**Purpose**

Extend Module 6 Lesson 6.1 with worked examples of minimum visibility sets for different business types, with a focus on cash timing and obligations calendars.

**Course outcomes:** M6.1 Identify a minimum visibility set

**Linked modules:** Seeing what is happening, and choosing a first improvement; What operations is, and why it is commercial

**Suggested format**

Three short examples (product, service, solo founder with contractors), then learners draft their own visibility set, then review in pairs.

**Practical takeaway**

A visibility set of up to eight signals with owners and frequency, and a list of obligations to confirm with their accountant or adviser.

**Specialist guest**

An accountant or bookkeeper experienced with small businesses would add value on cash timing and the obligations they typically see founders miss. Any jurisdiction-specific content to be marked as such. (Would add value)

## Worked case: diagnosing a missed-deadline problem

> Status: Proposal · no date agreed

**Purpose**

Practise the full diagnostic approach on a different case from the capstone, so learners rehearse the method without being given capstone answers.

**Course outcomes:** M2.3 Locate where work waits; M3.2 Ask diagnostic questions; M6.2 Prioritise a first improvement; M6.3 Define owner, measure, review and stop rule

**Linked modules:** How work moves through a business; Why founders become the bottleneck; Seeing what is happening, and choosing a first improvement

**Suggested format**

A short case in a different sector (for example, a small events business) is worked through live: diagnosis, questions, missing information, first priority, measure. Learners vote on priorities and defend choices.

**Practical takeaway**

A rehearsed method for the capstone and their own business, and the experience of seeing several defensible answers.

**Specialist guest**

Not needed. (Not needed)
