# Module handbook: Operations Fundamentals

_Operations Academy (working name). Exported from Nexa OS on 21 October 2026. Current drafts: nothing is approved unless its status says so._

> Status: Draft (not approved)

**Aim**

Give founders a shared operational vocabulary and enough judgement to diagnose why work depends on them, ask better questions, and choose a sensible first improvement.

The course answers one question: **how do you remove yourself as the bottleneck in productivity?** It does not answer it for each learner's business. It gives them the concepts and methods to answer it for themselves, and to judge advice and tools they are offered.

**Audience**

Founders and owner-managers of small and growing businesses, from sole traders with a few contractors to companies of around 50 people, who may have little formal operations knowledge. Intelligent, busy, experienced in their own field. No shared operational vocabulary is assumed.

## OF101 What operations is, and why it is commercial

**Principle**

Operations is how the business keeps its promises, reliably and at a cost it can afford.

**Method**

Trace each important promise through the work, the people and the money it depends on: capacity, cost and cash timing.

**Tactics**

1. List the three to five promises customers would complain about if you broke them.
2. Note the main constraint on each.
3. List the decisions that come up every month and mark the ones that need you.
4. Trace one promise: who does what, what it costs, when cash goes out and comes in.

**Coaching questions**

- Have you thought about which promise would break first if you were away for two weeks?
- Consider whether the price of your most common promise covers the time it really takes.
- Have you checked when the cash for that promise arrives, compared with when you pay for it?

**Learning outcomes**

- M1.1: Explain, in their own words, what operations covers in their business, giving one example each of a customer promise, a constraint and a recurring decision.
- M1.2: Trace how one customer promise affects delivery capacity, margin and the timing of cash in and out.

## OF102 How work moves through a business

**Principle**

Most delay is waiting, not working.

**Method**

Follow real work from trigger to result, and find where it waits and for whom, before deciding what to change.

**Tactics**

1. Choose one workflow tied to a promise.
2. Follow two or three real examples, including one that went badly.
3. Record each step: who, input, output, what it waits for and for how long.
4. Label it recurring, project or exception.
5. Answer the five pattern questions. Write questions, not solutions.

**Coaching questions**

- Have you followed one piece of work from start to finish, or are you describing it from memory?
- Consider where that work waits, and who it waits for.
- Have you thought about which approval exists because of a problem that no longer happens?

**Learning outcomes**

- M2.1: Map one workflow from trigger to completion, marking each handoff, queue, decision point and approval.
- M2.2: Distinguish recurring work, project work and exceptions in a given situation, and explain why each needs a different approach.
- M2.3: Identify where work waits, and for whom, using observed evidence rather than impression.

## OF103 Why founders become the bottleneck

**Principle**

"Everything comes to me" is several problems, and each one needs a different fix.

**Method**

Separate decision, knowledge, quality-check, relationship and visibility bottlenecks using evidence, then ask before you fix.

**Tactics**

1. For each bottleneck type, note whether you see it and the evidence.
2. Write three diagnostic questions for the two strongest.
3. List the information you don't have yet.
4. Mark any bottleneck with legal, safety or financial consequences, and decide who should change it.

**Coaching questions**

- Have you thought about which of the five bottlenecks you are, and which you are not?
- Consider what would happen if, for one week, you said "you decide" on every decision under an agreed amount.
- Have you thought about what a new hire would need from you so they don't become one more person waiting?

**Learning outcomes**

- M3.1: Diagnose which bottleneck types (decision, knowledge, quality check, relationship, visibility) are operating in a scenario, citing evidence for each.
- M3.2: Write diagnostic questions that separate a symptom from its likely causes and identify what information is missing before deciding.

## OF104 Ownership, decisions and when to get advice

**Principle**

Delegate outcomes and the authority to decide, not just tasks. Know where your own judgement ends.

**Method**

Set decide, consult and inform for recurring decisions, with thresholds and defined escalation. Check commitments before they're made, and brief specialists well.

**Tactics**

1. Build a five-row decision rights table for the decisions that interrupt you most.
2. Set thresholds and say when, to whom and how fast things escalate.
3. Use the commitment check before agreeing anything non-standard.
4. Keep five-line records of decisions that matter.
5. Use the advice brief when a specialist is needed.

**Coaching questions**

- Have you thought about which decision you make most often that someone else could make?
- Consider who agreed the last promise your team struggled to keep, and whether they were allowed to.
- Have you thought about what you would need to bring a solicitor or accountant to get a useful answer in one conversation?

**Learning outcomes**

- M4.1: For a recurring decision or outcome, specify the owner, their decision rights, an approval threshold and the escalation route.
- M4.2: Before a commitment is made, identify its consequences for scope, capacity, margin, payment and cash, and dependencies on suppliers or other customers.
- M4.3: Recognise when a matter needs a qualified adviser, say what kind, and list the information to gather before asking.

## OF105 Documentation and SOPs that people use

**Principle**

Document what works, give it an owner and keep it current. A document cannot fix a broken process.

**Method**

Decide whether a task needs documenting, design the process before writing it down, choose the lightest format, and judge tools by the job they need to do.

**Tactics**

1. Sort five recurring tasks with the "Should this be documented?" guide.
2. Fix what is obviously broken before you write.
3. Draft one SOP with an owner, access and a review date.
4. Have someone else follow it while you watch, then fix it.
5. Score your hub or tool against the job.

**Coaching questions**

- Have you thought about whether you need a document, or a decision?
- Consider whether the problem would go away if this SOP were followed perfectly.
- Have you thought about who will keep this document right in six months' time?

**Learning outcomes**

- M5.1: Decide whether a task needs documentation and what kind (none, checklist, SOP, guide, policy or template), using stated criteria.
- M5.2: Distinguish capturing current steps from designing a workable process, and explain what an SOP cannot fix in a given situation.
- M5.3: Evaluate an SOP hub or documentation tool against the job it needs to do, including ownership, access, review and the effort of keeping it current.

## OF106 Seeing what is happening, and choosing a first improvement

**Principle**

You can stop checking when the right people can see what matters. Change one thing at a time, and prove it worked.

**Method**

Agree a minimum visibility set. Prioritise improvements by impact, frequency, risk and effort. Give each one an owner, a measure, a review date and a stop rule.

**Tactics**

1. Write up to eight signals across delivery, money, capacity and obligations, each with an owner.
2. Score three to five candidate improvements.
3. Choose one and write the brief: owner, change, baseline, measure, review date, stop rule.
4. Review it, and say what the result does and does not show.

**Coaching questions**

- Have you thought about which three numbers would let you stop checking?
- Consider which single change would give you back the most hours a month.
- Have you decided what would make you stop or reverse this change?

**Learning outcomes**

- M6.1: Identify the minimum information needed to see whether delivery, money, capacity and obligations are on track, and who should see it and how often.
- M6.2: Prioritise a first improvement from several candidates using impact, frequency, risk and effort, and justify the choice.
- M6.3: Define an owner, a baseline and measure, a review date and a stop rule for an improvement, and state what success would and would not show.
