## Capstone: Kestrel Signs keeps missing deadlines

### Rubric v1 (current): First draft rubric for review and calibration in the pilot.

| Criterion | Required at Secure | Outcomes | Not yet evident | Developing | Secure | Strong |

| Diagnosis | Yes | M1.1 Explain what operations covers; M2.1 Map a workflow; M2.3 Locate where work waits; M3.1 Diagnose bottleneck types with evidence; M5.2 Distinguish capturing steps from designing a process | Restates symptoms or Rachel's view as the cause. No use of course concepts. | Identifies some causes using course concepts, but mixes facts and assumptions or misses a major factor (for example, the promise made without a capacity check, or the proof queue). | Identifies several plausible causes across workflow, bottleneck types, ownership and capacity, supported by the facts, and separates facts from suspicion. | As Secure, and explains how the causes interact (for example, why existing SOPs cannot prevent these failures) and what evidence would distinguish between competing explanations. |

| Feasibility | No | M2.2 Distinguish recurring work, projects and exceptions; M5.1 Decide whether and how to document; M6.2 Prioritise a first improvement | Proposals are unrealistic for a 12-person business (for example, a full system rollout or rewriting everything first). | Mostly realistic, but ignores a key constraint (one printer, part-time finance, a new coordinator). | Proportionate to the business and its constraints; treats tools and documentation as means, justified by the job they need to do. | As Secure, and anticipates the side effects of the proposed changes and how to limit them. |

**Standard**

**Meets the standard** when Diagnosis, Prioritisation, Ownership and decision rights, and Recognition of specialist advice needs are each at least Secure, and no criterion is Not yet evident. Otherwise the result is **Not yet** and a revision is requested.

**Deliberately incomplete information (assessor reference, not shown to learners)**

**Assessor reference. Not shown to learners.** The case deliberately omits the information below. Learners are told only that some information they would want is missing. Credit learners who identify these or other reasonable gaps, explain why each matters, and say how they would obtain it. Do not penalise a learner for missing one item if their overall identification of gaps is sound.

- Where the time actually goes on a late job (no timestamps are kept).
- Whether "5 working days from order" starts at order or at artwork approval. The agreement wording has not been checked.
- The exact service credit terms, and whether any are already payable.
- The margin on Brightway work compared with other customers.
- How many hours the printer and installers are actually available each week, against demand.
- Whether the SOPs are followed, partly followed or ignored, and why.
- Who, besides Rachel, is allowed to approve a proof, change a job or agree a delivery date.
- What Brightway's operations manager actually considers most important.
- Dev's employment status, insurance and any training or certification relevant to the work, and what Kestrel's own insurance requires.

**Assessor guidance**

**What you are assessing.** Applied judgement about a hypothetical business: diagnosis, questions, recognition of missing information, prioritisation, ownership, feasibility, commercial and governance awareness, recognition of specialist advice needs, and measurement. You are not assessing writing style, presentation polish or knowledge of any tool.

**Multiple defensible answers.** Learners may reasonably start in different places: with the proof sign-off queue, with clarifying the contract's start point and service credits, with getting basic timing data, or with an urgent conversation with Brightway. Credit any starting point that is justified by the facts and the learner's stated reasoning. Do not reward agreement with your own preferred approach.

**What strong responses tend to do** (examples, not a checklist):
- Notice that the SOPs exist but cannot fix a promise that may exceed capacity, a proof queue at the founder, missing decision rights on job changes, or a single printer.
- Separate facts from assumptions, and propose ways to get missing data, such as following the next few jobs and noting where they wait.
- Recognise the decision, quality-check, relationship and visibility bottlenecks around Rachel.
- Recognise that the 5-day promise was made without a capacity check, and that 60-day terms on the largest customer plus delayed invoicing affect cash.
- Flag the agreement (start point, service credits) for review by a solicitor, and Dev's status, insurance and work at height for appropriate specialist input, without stating what the law requires.
- Treat "a proper project management system" as a hypothesis to test, not a starting point.
- Give the first change an owner other than Rachel where sensible, a baseline, a measure, a review date and a stop rule.

**Common weaknesses:**
- Jumping straight to a tool or a rewrite of all SOPs.
- Confident claims about legal requirements. Recognising a likely issue is good; asserting the law is not what is assessed and should not be rewarded.
- Treating Rachel's view ("people don't follow the SOPs") as established fact.
- Ten simultaneous priorities.
- Ownership assigned to "the team".
- Measures with no baseline or review.

**Coverage.** The capstone assesses outcomes M1.1 to M6.3 except M5.3 (evaluating a hub or tool), which is assessed through Exercise 5. A learner who proposes a tool should justify it by the job it needs to do; credit that under Feasibility.

**Time.** Allow about 45 to 60 minutes for a first assessment and 20 to 30 minutes for a resubmission (estimates).

**Hypothetical reasoning, not implementation.** A "Meets the standard" result shows reasoning about a fictional case. Record it that way. It is not evidence that the learner has changed their own business.

**AI-assisted feedback.** Not used in this MVP. If introduced later, it must be optional, clearly labelled, and unable to set a result without assessor approval.

**Feedback prompts**

Use two or three of these per criterion, adapted to the submission.

- "Your diagnosis of ___ is well supported by ___. What evidence would tell you whether ___ is also contributing?"
- "You've assumed ___. How would you check that before acting on it?"
- "Which of your questions would you ask first, and of whom?"
- "What would happen to your plan if the agreement turned out to start the 5 days at artwork approval?"
- "You've given ___ to Rachel. What would need to be true for someone else to own it?"
- "How would you know in four weeks whether this had helped? What is the baseline?"
- "What would make you stop or reverse this change?"
- "Which part of this needs a qualified adviser, and what would you take to them?"
- "You've proposed ___ changes at once. Which one would you start with if you could only do one, and why?"

**Resubmission process**

1. If any criterion is "Not yet evident", or the standard is not met, the assessor records criterion-level feedback, sets the status to **Revision requested**, and gives the learner two or three specific prompts.
2. The learner may resubmit once within four weeks (estimate; to be agreed). They revise only what they need to, and add a short note (up to 200 words) explaining what they changed and why.
3. The resubmission is recorded as a new attempt linked to the first. The first attempt, its feedback and the rubric version used are kept unchanged.
4. The assessor reviews against the **same rubric version** used for the first attempt unless the learner and assessor agree otherwise, and records that choice.
5. A second "Not yet" leads to a conversation, not a third automatic attempt. The programme owner decides the next step.

**What this does and does not show**

This assessment shows applied reasoning about a hypothetical business. It does not show that the learner has implemented changes in their own business or that any change worked. No certificate or accreditation is attached to completion.

## POSI: Proof of Skill Implementation

### Rubric v1 (current): First draft POSI rubric for review and calibration in the pilot.

| Criterion | Required at Secure | Outcomes | Not yet evident | Partly evidenced | Evidenced | Strong |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Diagnosis grounded in evidence | No | M2.1 Map a workflow; M3.1 Diagnose bottleneck types with evidence | No evidence of the problem, or the problem is assumed. | Some evidence, but it doesn't clearly show where work waited or why. | Credible evidence of where work waited and which bottleneck type it was. | As Evidenced, and the learner tested their first explanation and revised it. |
| Choice of change | No | M6.2 Prioritise a first improvement | No reason given for this change. | A reason, but no comparison with alternatives. | Justified by impact, frequency, risk and effort, with alternatives considered. | As Evidenced, and the learner explains what they deliberately left for later and why. |
| Ownership moved | Yes | M4.1 Specify owner, decision rights, threshold and escalation; M6.3 Define owner, measure, review and stop rule | Ownership stayed with the founder, or is unclear. | A person is named but their decision rights or limits are unclear. | A named owner with clear decision rights, a threshold and an escalation route. | As Evidenced, and the learner checked the owner had the capability, information and time. |
| What was put in place | No | M5.1 Decide whether and how to document | Nothing put in place, or only a plan. | Something put in place, but it doesn't match what the change needed. | Proportionate artefacts (for example a checklist, SOP, threshold table or signal) with an owner and review date. | As Evidenced, and the artefacts were tested with the person using them and improved. |
| Founder stepped out | Yes | M4.1 Specify owner, decision rights, threshold and escalation | No evidence the founder stopped doing, deciding or checking anything. | Stepped back briefly, or only partly. | Stopped for at least two weeks, except through the agreed escalation route. | As Evidenced, and the learner describes what they did when tempted to step back in. |

**Standard**

**Evidenced** when Ownership moved, Founder stepped out and Measurement are each at least Evidenced, and no criterion is Not yet evident. Otherwise the result is **In progress** and the learner is invited to add evidence.

**Assessor guidance**

**What you are assessing.** That a real change happened, that ownership and decision rights genuinely moved away from the founder, and that the learner can judge whether it worked. Not the scale of the result.

**Accept small, real changes.** Moving refund approvals under £200 to a team lead, with a weekly list for the founder, is a good POSI. A grand redesign that never left the planning stage is not.

**Credibility, not proof.** You can't verify everything. Look for consistency between the problem, the artefacts, the measure and the reflection. Ask questions where it doesn't add up.

**Specialist matters.** If the change touched legal, safety, financial or regulatory obligations, look for evidence the learner recognised it and got appropriate input. Do not assess whether they met any specific requirement.

**What a pass means.** "Evidenced" means the assessor has seen credible evidence of one implemented change and the learner's judgement about it. It is not a guarantee of business results, and no certificate or accreditation is attached.

**Feedback prompts**

- "Who owns this now, and what can they decide without you?"
- "What did you stop doing, and what happened the first time you didn't step in?"
- "Your baseline was ___. What else could explain the change you saw?"
- "What would make you take this back? Has that happened?"
- "Consider what you'd hand over next, and what you'd do differently."

**Resubmission process**

If the change hasn't had time to hold, or ownership hasn't really moved, the result is **In progress** with specific prompts. The learner can add evidence and resubmit within eight weeks (estimate; to be agreed). Earlier submissions and feedback are kept.

**What this does and does not show**

POSI is evidence of implementation in the learner's own business, self-selected and possibly anonymised. It is assessed for credibility and judgement. It does not prove business outcomes, and no certificate or accreditation is attached.
