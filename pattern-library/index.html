The Pattern Library.

68 named patterns behind how people and organisations behave, drawn from nine fields, from behavioural economics to military doctrine. Each one is graded on the strength of its evidence, because a good story is not the same as a finding.

Behavioural economics · Well known Contested

Choice overload (the paradox of choice)

With many options, people sometimes choose less or regret more.

Behavioural economics · Well known Contested

Escalation of commitment

We keep investing in a failing course of action because we chose it, and stopping feels like admitting the mistake.

Behavioural economics · Well known Contested

Loss aversion

People judge outcomes as gains or losses against a reference point, and on average a loss weighs more than a gain of the same size.

Behavioural economics · Niche Contested

Satisficing

Setting an aspiration level and taking the first option that meets it, instead of searching for the best possible option.

Behavioural economics · Well known Robust

Status quo bias

People stick with the current option, or whatever is pre-selected, more often than its merits justify.

Behavioural economics · Niche Robust

Taboo trade-offs and sacred values

People treat some values (loyalty, identity, the founding mission) as sacred and refuse to weigh them against money or convenience.

Behavioural economics · Well known Contested

The endowment effect

People demand more to give up something they own than they would pay to get it.

Behavioural economics · Niche Contested

The hiding hand

Hirschman argued we underestimate both how hard projects are and how inventive we'll be, and the two errors can cancel out.

Cognitive bias · Well known Robust

Availability heuristic

People judge how likely something is by how easily examples come to mind.

Cognitive bias · Well known Robust

Halo effect

A strong overall impression of a person or company colours judgements of their separate, unrelated qualities.

Cognitive bias · Well known Robust

Hindsight bias (creeping determinism)

Once people know an outcome, they judge it as more predictable than it was and misremember their earlier forecasts.

Cognitive bias · Niche Robust

Illusion of explanatory depth

We think we understand how things work far better than we do, and only notice when we try to explain the mechanism step by step.

Cognitive bias · Well known Robust

Planning fallacy

We underestimate how long our own projects will take, because we plan from the details in front of us and ignore how similar projects went.

Cognitive bias · Niche Contested

Prospective hindsight and the pre-mortem

Explaining a future event as if it has already happened, to surface reasons a plan could fail.

Cognitive bias · Well known Robust

Survivorship bias

Drawing conclusions from the cases that made it through while the failures stay invisible.

Cognitive bias · Well known Contested

The Dunning-Kruger effect

In a 1999 study the weakest performers overestimated their results most, because judging a skill takes the same skill.

History and anthropology · Story Contested

Diamond's Collapse told of islanders who deforested Rapa Nui to move statues and then fell; later research disputes most of it.

History and anthropology · Niche Anecdotal

Schismogenesis

Two parties respond to each other's behaviour in a way that pushes both further apart over time.

History and anthropology · Story Folk wisdom

The cobra effect

An incentive tied to a proxy makes the real problem worse, because people produce the proxy instead of the outcome.

History and anthropology · Niche Robust

The gift and the obligation to reciprocate

Mauss argued that gifts are never free: they carry obligations to give, receive and reciprocate, and refusing any of them is a rupture.

History and anthropology · Story Folk wisdom

In 1847 Semmelweis showed handwashing cut childbed fever deaths, and the profession rejected the evidence.

History and anthropology · Story Anecdotal

History and anthropology · Niche Anecdotal

Ubuntu (umuntu ngumuntu ngabantu)

A southern African ethic: a person is a person through other people.
